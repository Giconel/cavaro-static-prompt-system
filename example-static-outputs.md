# Example Static Outputs

Two worked examples showing the full 5-option output format. Both use placeholder products. Use these as reference for structure, depth, and tone.

---

## Example 1

**Input — Product:** Relaxed-fit knit cardigan in warm taupe. Ribbed texture throughout. Open front, no buttons. Long straight sleeves with ribbed cuffs. Hip length. V-neck. Medium weight. Relaxed drape.

**Input — Angle:** Comfort Without Looking Sloppy

---

### 1. Quick Visual Read

- **Category:** Knit cardigan
- **Colour:** Warm taupe
- **Fabric / Texture:** Ribbed knit, medium weight, relaxed drape
- **Fit:** Relaxed
- **Silhouette:** Open front, straight
- **Neckline:** V-neck
- **Sleeves:** Long, straight, ribbed cuffs
- **Closures:** None — open front
- **Length:** Hip
- **Pattern / Print:** Solid
- **Other Details:** Ribbed texture throughout including hem and cuffs
- **Product accuracy risks:** Image generator may smooth out ribbing texture, shorten the length, or close the open front — all must be preserved
- **Selected angle:** Comfort Without Looking Sloppy
- **What the angle must communicate visually:** She put this on and looked effortlessly put-together without trying

---

### 2. Static Strategy

- **Main creative hypothesis:** A relaxed knit cardigan can look polished in everyday Australian life — not like loungewear
- **What must be proven in the image:** The cardigan must read as comfortable AND intentional — the viewer must believe it is a real outfit choice, not something she grabbed on the way out the door
- **What must be avoided:** Anything that makes her look like she is in pyjamas, recovering from a cold, or working from home — no couch, no living room, no overly casual slouch

---
