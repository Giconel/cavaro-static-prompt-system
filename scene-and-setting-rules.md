# Scene and Setting Rules

## Core Principle

The setting must feel real. It must be a place an Australian woman aged 35–55 would actually be on an ordinary day. The setting supports the angle — it does not compete with the product.

---

## Approved Settings

Use these settings as your primary options. Match the setting to the angle and product type.

**Inner-city café strip**
- Footpath outside a café with outdoor seating
- Warm stone pavement, timber chairs, coffee cups, potted plants
- Good for: elevated everyday angles, socialising, morning routines

**Suburban high street**
- A local shopping strip with mixed shopfronts
- Footpath, parked cars, awnings, street greenery
- Good for: everyday errands, practical angles, easy outfit styling

**Weekend farmers market**
- Outdoor market with canvas stalls, produce, other shoppers in soft background
- Natural morning light, casual crowd
- Good for: weekend ease, comfort angles, seasonal product

**Beachside esplanade**
- Coastal path or promenade, water visible in the background
- Soft morning light, natural greenery, people in the distance
- Good for: relaxed lifestyle, summer or transitional product, freedom angles

**Residential street with established gardens**
- A leafy suburban street, brick or weatherboard homes visible in background
- Dappled light through trees, footpath, parked cars
- Good for: everyday polish, home and lifestyle angles, afternoon outings

**Local shopping village**
- A boutique-style shopping area — not a big mall, more of a village strip
- Timber shopfronts, flowers, coffee in hand, relaxed pace
- Good for: weekend outings, treat yourself angles, lifestyle ease

**City laneway**
- A Melbourne or Sydney-style laneway with brick walls, small café or shopfront
- Good for: slightly more urban, polished-casual angle, city lifestyle

---

## How To Choose The Right Setting

Match the setting to the angle using this logic:

| Angle type | Suggested setting |
|---|---|
| Comfort and ease | Farmers market, residential street, suburb high street |
| Weekend lifestyle | Farmers market, beachside esplanade, shopping village |
| Everyday polish | Café strip, city laneway, shopping village |
| Getting dressed simply | Residential street, suburb high street |
| Social and outgoing | Café strip, laneway, shopping village |
| Relaxed and coastal | Beachside esplanade, residential street |

---

## Background Rules

The background is not decoration. It is part of the believability of the image. A bad background destroys trust.

**Required depth and texture:**
- The background must have real depth — not flat, not blurred to white
- Use realistic surface textures: worn concrete pavement, aged brick, weathered timber, natural stone, painted render, café tiles
- The background should feel lived-in, not dressed

**Environmental details to include:**
- Parked cars or bicycles further down the street
- Distant people or soft crowd movement in the background
- Café furniture, market stalls, shopfront awnings, or window displays
- Australian native plants — grevillea, banksia, bottlebrush — or established deciduous garden trees
- Street signs, bins, poles, or other ordinary urban elements that confirm a real location

**Australian architecture signals:**
- Brick and weatherboard homes with established gardens
- Federation-era shopfronts with timber awnings
- Rendered or tiled café and retail facades
- Wide suburban footpaths with nature strips
- Coastal path railings, timber boardwalks, or esplanade infrastructure

**Light and time of day:**
- Morning light (8am–11am) is the default — warm, soft, and flattering
- Slightly overcast morning light works well — even, no harsh shadows
- Avoid midday overhead light (too harsh) and late afternoon golden hour (too editorial)
- Avoid night or indoor-only scenes

---

## What To Avoid

- Luxury hotel lobbies or resorts
- Empty streets that look like a set
- Generic greenery with no location feel
- Fake studio backgrounds
- Tropical or overseas-looking locations
- Overly clean or art-directed environments
- Backgrounds that compete with the product for attention
